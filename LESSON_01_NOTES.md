## 2. Linguistic Diagnosis: Strengths & Priority Upgrades

### 🌟 Key Strengths Observed
1. **Analytical Listening & Rhythm Awareness:** During the *"Cats Eat Mice"* exercise, you immediately spotted the two exact mechanics of native English speech: linking words together (*"The cats"* as one unit) and grouping sentences into rhythmic chunks.
2. **Willingness to Explore Abstract Topics:** When describing Tadao Ando's *Church of Light*, even when you didn't know a specific architectural or religious term (*crucifix*, *priest*, *congregation*, *concrete*), you paraphrased effectively and kept the conversation moving.
3. **Great Conversational Warmth:** You naturally ask follow-up questions (*"Where are you from?"*, *"How is life there?"*, *"With a motorbike or by car?"*), which is the foundation of great **small talk** and **networking**.

### 🎯 Core Areas We Will Upgrade Together
1. **From Syllable-Timed (Spanish) to Stress-Timed (English) Rhythm:**
   - In Spanish, every syllable takes roughly the same time. In English, we stress the **anchor content words** and compress ("cheat" / eat) the grammar words in between so a 7-word sentence takes almost the same time as a 3-word sentence.
   - **Line 4 Breakthrough:** `"The CATS ... might've ea'n ... the MICE"` (compressing *might have* $\rightarrow$ `/ˈmaɪtəv/` and softening the `/t/` in *eaten* $\rightarrow$ `/ˈiːʔn/`).
2. **Monthly Business Analyst Presentation Drills:**
   - Moving from *"explaining what I'm doing"* on the spot to crisp, executive signposting when presenting past accomplishments, current sprint priorities, and process **bottlenecks**.
3. **High-Pressure Verbal Agility:**
   - In upcoming lessons, we will use **asymmetrical communication drills** (like *Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes*) to train your brain to describe shapes, sequences (*descending order*), and processes calmly under time pressure.

## 3. Core Vocabulary Explicitly Taught in Lesson 01

| Word / Term | IPA (UK) | Category | Meaning & How We Used It in Class |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. A gathering** *(noun)* | `/ˈɡæð.ər.ɪŋ/` | Social & Work | **An informal meeting or social get-together.** While *to gather* can mean "to collect things", saying *"I'm gathering things"* is unnatural. Native speakers use **a gathering** for your weekly remote catch-up with fellow Business Analysts or Friday climbing + burgers & beers with friends! |
| **2. A bottleneck** *(noun)* | `/ˈbɒt.əl.nek/` | Business & BA | **A point of congestion in a system or workflow that slows everything else down** (*cuello de botella*). E.g., when Marketing can't keep up with Operations, polishing can't keep up with welding, or HR can't train/onboard new staff fast enough. |
| **3. Asymmetrical** *(adj.)* | `/ˌeɪ.sɪˈmet.rɪ.kəl/` | Business & Games | **Having two sides or halves that are unequal or different** (*asimétrico*). In our game *Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes*, the information is asymmetrical: one person sees the bomb, the other has the manual. |
| **4. Monolithic** *(adj.)* / **Monolith** *(n.)* | `/ˌmɒn.əˈlɪθ.ɪk/` | Architecture & Scale | **Massive, solid, singular, and unified in scale** (like the monolith in *2001: A Space Odyssey*). Used to describe the commanding concrete wall at the back of Tadao Ando's church. |
| **5. Functional** *(adj.)* | `/ˈfʌŋk.ʃən.əl/` | Design & Business | **Practical and designed to serve a clear purpose**, rather than being **fancy** or **elaborate**. E.g., The seating in the *Church of Light* is simple and functional so nothing distracts from prayer. |
| **6. Congregation** *(noun)* | `/ˌkɒŋ.ɡrɪˈɡeɪ.ʃən/` | Culture & Space | **The group of people gathered for a religious service in a church.** Taught alongside **Crucifix** (`/ˈkruː.sɪ.fɪks/`) and **Priest / Reverend** (`/priːst/`, `/ˈrev.ər.ənd/`). |
| **7. Spot** *(noun — UK/US)* | `/spɒt/` | Everyday Small Talk | **A casual, natural word for a place, space, or local restaurant.** E.g., *"Where's your favourite spot in the mountains?"* or *"Did you try that new burger spot on Friday?"* |
| **8. Tranquil** *(adj.)* | `/ˈtræŋ.kwɪl/` | Nature & Mood | **Peacefully quiet and calm, yet gently alive with nature.** Hearing a mountain stream running, birds singing, and wind brushing through the trees is **tranquil** (unlike a dead-silent forest or noisy Madrid traffic!). |

## 4. "What Miguel Said" $\rightarrow$ Natural British & Executive Upgrades

| Context | What You Said in Class | Polished Native / Business Upgrade | Why This Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Goals** | *"Improve my frequency / influency... and the small dog in everyday life."* | **"I want to improve my fluency (`/ˈfluː.ən.si/`), pronunciation (`/prəˌnʌn.siˈeɪ.ʃən/`), and everyday small talk."** | Watch the pronunciation of **fluency** (not *frequency*) and **pronunciation** (`-NUN-`, not `-NOUN-`). |
| **2. Hobbies** | *"I'm a young person that likes a lot of practicing sports... improve my client in the ability."* | **"I'm really into sports—Right now I'm working on improving my indoor climbing ability."** | Avoid *"I like a lot of practicing..."* Use **"I'm really into [sport]"** or **"I love playing sports / climbing."** |
| **3. Tech Audio** | *"It was taking the micro of the camera."* | **"It was picking up the built-in webcam mic (`/maɪk/`) / microphone."** | False friend alert! In Spanish we say *el micro*, but in English we say **the mic** (`/maɪk/`) or **microphone**. |
| **4. Experience** | *"This is my fifty year... I was working in our company until five months ago."* | **"I'm in my fifth (`/fɪfθ/`) year of working, and I worked at another company until five months ago."** | Distinguish **fifth** (`/fɪfθ/` = 5th) from **fiftieth** (50th), and **another / my previous company** from *our company*. |
| **5. Meetings** | *"Once, twice a month I have to present a meeting where I explain what I'm doing."* | **"Once or twice a month, I give a presentation in a meeting to walk the team through my monthly progress."** | We **give / deliver a presentation** or **present IN a meeting** (never *"present a meeting"*). |
| **6. Vietnam** | *"So you nowadays you are living there in Vietnam?"* | **"So are you living in Vietnam these days?"** / **"Are you based in Vietnam now?"** | *Nowadays* is used for broad general trends (*"Nowadays people work remotely"*); for one person's life, use **these days** or **currently**. |
| **7. Materials** | *"The floor and seats seem like something similar to good [wood]... on the back of the room."* | **"The floor and pews look like dark wood (`/wʊd/`), while the walls at the back of the room are raw concrete (`/ˈkɒŋ.kriːt/`)."** | Round your lips for `/w/` in **wood** (`/wʊd/`) so it doesn't sound like *good* (`/ɡʊd/`), and say **at the back of the room**. |
| **8. Church** | *"For the people who attend to the church or the person who is giving the church."* | **"For the congregation attending the service, or the priest leading the service."** | In English, **attend** takes no preposition when it means "go to": **attend a meeting / attend church** (*attend to* means "deal with/take care of"). |
| **9. Madrid Stress** | *"The everyday life is distressful... The people is going crazy."* | **"Everyday city life is quite stressful / hectic. People are driving like crazy!"** | Use **stressful / hectic** for busy city life (*distressing* = emotionally traumatic). Also: **People ARE** (always plural in English!). |
| **10. Safety** | *"You have to be so alert in order not to have any accident."* | **"On a motorbike, you have to stay super alert to avoid accidents."** | **"Stay alert to avoid accidents"** is crisp, natural, and conversational. |
